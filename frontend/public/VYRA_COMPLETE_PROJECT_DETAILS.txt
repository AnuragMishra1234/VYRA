1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & RESEARCH MOTIVATION
================================================================================
1.1 The Navigation Dilemma: Reactive Switching vs Continuous Drift
Autonomous vehicles, mobile robots, and intelligent transportation systems require
uninterrupted, lane-level horizontal positioning (< 1.0 m to 5.0 m accuracy) to navigate
safely. Contemporary localization architectures combine Global Navigation Satellite
Systems (GNSS) with Onboard Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and wheel odometry using
Extended Kalman Filters (EKF).

However, in dense urban canyons, highway underpasses, covered interchanges, and tree-lined
boulevards, GNSS signals experience non-line-of-sight (NLOS) reception, multipath reflections,
and total satellite occlusion. In these compromised zones, navigation systems face a
fundamental dilemma:
1. "Switching Too Late": If the EKF continues fusing degraded GNSS measurements whose
   pseudorange biases range from 10 m to 50+ m, spurious observation updates rapidly corrupt
   the filter state, introducing catastrophic position divergence.
2. "Switching Too Early": If the system immediately disengages GNSS at the first sign of
   minor signal perturbation to enter Dead Reckoning (DR), open-loop integration of IMU
   accelerometer noise and gyroscope drift causes rapid, unbounded polynomial error expansion.

Conventional navigation systems address this through reactive, instantaneous threshold-based
switching (e.g., cut off GNSS if HDOP > 4.0 or satellite count < 4). These reactive approaches
suffer from acute latency: by the time an instantaneous metric crosses an alarm threshold,
corrupted fixes have already entered the navigation pipeline. Conversely, when signals hover
near the threshold boundary, reactive switches induce high-frequency "chattering" (rapid,
destabilizing mode toggling).

1.2 Core Research Question & Technical Philosophy
VYRA resolves this operational impasse by replacing reactive switching with forecast-driven,
closed-loop action-conditioned error prediction. Rather than asking:
   "Is the GNSS signal currently degraded?"
VYRA continuously poses the forward-looking counterfactual query:
   "If GNSS, HYBRID fusion, or pure Dead Reckoning is committed to at this instant, what
   maximum localization error and bound violation risk will occur over the next H seconds?"

1.3 Summary of Key Research Contributions
1. Closed-Loop Action-Conditioned Forecasting: Formulated and trained an action-conditioned
   gradient-boosted regressor that predicts the future maximum positioning error for candidate
   navigation actions over short horizons H in {1s, 3s, 5s, 10s}, reaching 95.43% top-1
   optimal mode ranking accuracy and an average decision regret of only 0.056 m.
2. Analytical Bi-Quadratic DR Survivability (T_surv): Derived a closed-form analytical
   solution for the maximum duration DR can be maintained before exceeding an operational
   safety bound (5.0 m) under a Rayleigh distribution, suppressing unnecessary handovers
   by 91.0% (from 178 down to 16).
3. Pre-Outage Warning with 3.65 s Lead Time: Developed a Platt-calibrated gradient-boosted
   predictor operating on a 21-dimensional causal feature vector that detects imminent GNSS
   degradation with AUC-ROC = 0.942 and a mean lead-time of 3.65 seconds prior to signal loss.
4. Comprehensive Empirical Validation: Validated on 170,243 real-world epochs (4.72 hours)
   from the IO-VNBD benchmark. Across 20 simulated outage sweeps on the held-out test route
   (V-S3a), VYRA achieved 0.399 m Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE), outperforming GNSS-Only
   (17.136 m, a 97.7% reduction), Pure DR (720.202 m, a 99.9% reduction), and conventional
   Reactive Switching (0.712 m, a 44.0% reduction with 51.5% fewer 5 m safety violations).

2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & END-TO-END PIPELINE FLOW
================================================================================
2.1 End-to-End Functional Topology
The VYRA framework operates as a 10 Hz synchronous closed loop comprising four tightly
integrated subsystems:

+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       1. SENSOR INGESTION & QUALITY ENGINE                      |
|  - WGS84/CAN/IMU Ingestion at 10 Hz                                             |
|  - WGS84 -> ECEF -> ENU Coordinate Projection                                   |
|  - Multi-Sensor Kinematic Consistency: S_sat(t), S_kin(t), S_stab(t)            |
|  - Continuous Composite Health Metric Q_t in [0.0, 1.0]                         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                       |
                                       v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    2. PREDICTIVE GNSS DEGRADATION DETECTOR                       |
|  - Causal 5.0 s Historical Window State Vector s_t in R^21                      |
|  - Platt-Calibrated Gradient Boosted Classifier                                 |
|  - Forward Impairment Probability P(Y(t, H)=1 | s_t) with 3.65 s Mean Lead Time |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                       |
                                       v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|              3. ANALYTICAL DR SURVIVABILITY & STATE PROPAGATION                 |
|  - 6-State Loosely-Coupled EKF (Position, Velocity, Yaw, Gyro Bias)             |
|  - Dynamic Measurement Covariance Inflation R_k(Q_t)                            |
|  - 4th-Order Polynomial Covariance Expansion sigma_pos^2(T)                     |
|  - Closed-Form Bi-Quadratic Median Survivable Duration T_surv                   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                       |
                                       v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 4. ACTION-CONDITIONED FORECAST & POLICY ENGINE                  |
|  - Bilinear Interaction Feature Vector x_cond in R^34                           |
|  - Huber-Trained Action-Conditioned Regressor e_hat(t, A, H)                    |
|  - Multi-Objective Decision Cost J(A) (Tracking Error + Risk + DR Penalty)      |
|  - Regularization: 0.5 m Hysteresis, 2.0 s Dwell-Time, 15 m Emergency Bypass   |
|  - Active Navigation Mode Selection M_t in {GNSS, HYBRID, DR}                   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

2.2 Candidate Action Space Definition
At each decision epoch t, the policy considers three mutually exclusive operational modes:
- Action A_1 = GNSS (One-hot [1, 0, 0]^T): Raw satellite fixes accepted directly.
- Action A_2 = HYBRID (One-hot [0, 1, 0]^T): 6-state loosely-coupled EKF with adaptive
  measurement noise covariance inflation R_k(Q_t).
- Action A_3 = DR (One-hot [0, 0, 1]^T): Open-loop strapdown dead reckoning using wheel
  odometry and yaw rate, completely isolating the navigation state from GNSS.

2.3 Closed-Loop Control and Decision Cycle (10 Hz)
Every delta_t = 0.1 seconds:
1. Raw GNSS fixes, CAN wheel speeds, and IMU angular rates/accelerations are acquired.
2. Coordinates are projected into the local tangent ENU plane; composite quality Q_t is evaluated.
3. Feature vector s_t is populated; degradation probability P(Y) is inferred.
4. Analytical DR survivability T_surv is computed via closed-form roots.
5. Action-conditioned feature vectors x_cond are constructed for all three actions.
6. Maximum error e_hat(A) and bound violation risk are predicted for each candidate.
7. Multi-objective cost J(A) is scored; mode handover occurs if hysteresis and dwell-time
   criteria are satisfied.
8. State estimate is output to the vehicle motion planner and real-time dashboard.

3. DATASET SPECIFICATIONS & ZERO-LEAKAGE PROTOCOL
================================================================================
3.1 IO-VNBD Benchmark Dataset Properties
The empirical foundation of VYRA is the Inertial Odometry and Vehicle Navigation Benchmark
Dataset (IO-VNBD), collected and released by Uche Onyekpeu et al. (Coventry University, UK):
- Target Series: Real-world vehicular driving series (V-Series).
- Sampling Frequency: Synchronized 10 Hz (delta_t = 0.1 s).
- Total Volume: 170,243 rows across 4.72 hours of authentic UK urban, suburban, and highway driving.
- Ground Truth Reference: High-precision dual-antenna RTK GNSS coupled with a tactical-grade
  Inertial Navigation System (INS), recording geodetic coordinates to 8 decimal places (~1.1 mm).

3.2 Sensor Modalities & Raw Measurement Channels
The recorded data streams comprise:
- GNSS: Geodetic Latitude [deg], Longitude [deg], Altitude [m], Ground Speed [km/h],
  Course Heading [deg], Satellites Visible [integer count].
- IMU: 3-axis Accelerations (longitudinal a_x, lateral a_y, vertical a_z) [m/s^2];
  3-axis Angular Rates (roll rate omega_x, pitch rate omega_y, yaw rate omega_z) [deg/s and rad/s].
- Vehicle CAN Bus: Front steering wheel angle [deg], 4-wheel speeds and transmission speed [km/h].

3.3 Strict Trajectory-Level Partitioning (V-S1, V-S2, V-S3a)
To guarantee rigorous generalization and prevent data leakage, dataset splitting is executed
strictly at the trajectory level (entire independent physical driving journeys):
- Training Split (V-S1): 51,194 epochs (85.3 minutes, 1.42 hours). Used exclusively for
  feature normalization parameter fitting, classifier training, and regressor training.
- Validation Split (V-S2): 94,428 epochs (157.4 minutes, 2.62 hours). Used exclusively for
  hyperparameter optimization, Platt probability calibration, and threshold selection.
- Held-Out Test Split (V-S3a): 24,621 epochs (41.0 minutes, 0.68 hours). Completely unseen
  during training and calibration; used solely for final closed-loop comparative evaluation.

3.4 Anti-Leakage Feature Scaling & Target Masking Protocol
1. Trajectory-Level Isolation: No segments of test trip V-S3a are present in V-S1 or V-S2.
2. Training-Only Scaling: All z-score scalers (mean, variance) and min-max boundaries are
   computed solely on V-S1 and frozen.
3. Strict Causal History Windows: Features extracted at epoch t utilize historical data
   strictly from [t - L, t] where L = 50 epochs (5.0 s). Future epochs (t > t_now) are
   strictly inaccessible to online inference.
4. Outage Target Masking: During simulated outages, operational measurement columns are masked
   before being supplied to feature extractors or estimators.

4. SPATIAL GEOMETRY & COORDINATE TRANSFORMATIONS
================================================================================
4.1 Geodetic WGS84 to Earth-Centered Earth-Fixed (ECEF)
Given geodetic latitude phi, longitude lambda, and ellipsoidal height h, coordinates are
projected to Cartesian ECEF coordinates (X, Y, Z) using the WGS84 reference ellipsoid
(semi-major axis a = 6,378,137.0 m; flattening f = 1 / 298.257223563; eccentricity e^2 = 2f - f^2):

   N(phi) = a / sqrt(1 - e^2 * sin^2(phi))

   X = (N(phi) + h) * cos(phi) * cos(lambda)
   Y = (N(phi) + h) * cos(phi) * sin(lambda)
   Z = (N(phi) * (1 - e^2) + h) * sin(phi)

4.2 ECEF to Local East-North-Up (ENU) Tangent Plane
To eliminate planetary curvature and establish an intuitive Cartesian navigation frame, ECEF
vectors are projected relative to a fixed trajectory origin (phi_0, lambda_0, h_0) into
East-North-Up coordinates (p_e, p_n, p_u):

   [ p_e ]   [ -sin(lambda_0)              cos(lambda_0)             0          ] [ X - X_0 ]
   [ p_n ] = [ -sin(phi_0)*cos(lambda_0)  -sin(phi_0)*sin(lambda_0)  cos(phi_0) ] [ Y - Y_0 ]
   [ p_u ]   [  cos(phi_0)*cos(lambda_0)   cos(phi_0)*sin(lambda_0)  sin(phi_0) ] [ Z - Z_0 ]

All horizontal localization errors in VYRA are computed on the 2D plane:
   e_pos(t) = sqrt((p_e(t) - p_e,ref(t))^2 + (p_n(t) - p_n,ref(t))^2)

4.3 Vehicle Compass Heading to ENU Yaw Angle
IO-VNBD compass course theta_comp (measured clockwise from Geographic North, 0 deg = North,
90 deg = East) is mapped into the standard ENU mathematical orientation psi (counter-clockwise
from East, 0 rad = East, pi/2 rad = North):

   psi = (450 deg - theta_comp) mod 360 deg
   psi_rad = psi * (pi / 180)


================================================================================
5. GNSS QUALITY ESTIMATION ENGINE
================================================================================
5.1 Satellite Constellation Geometry Score (S_sat)
Reflects geometric satellite availability normalized between minimum fix requirements and
nominal open-sky visibility:

   S_sat(t) = clip( (N_sat(t) - N_min) / (N_nominal - N_min), 0.0, 1.0 )

   Parameters: N_min = 4 (minimum for 3D trilateration); N_nominal = 12 (nominal line-of-sight).

5.2 Kinematic Velocity Discrepancy & Consistency Score (S_kin)
Detects pseudorange multipath and Doppler velocity corruption by measuring the physical
discrepancy between GNSS Doppler speed and CAN wheel-speed odometry:

   D_kin(t) = | v_gnss(t) - v_wheel(t) |
   S_kin(t) = exp( - D_kin(t)^2 / (2 * sigma_kin^2) )

   Parameter: sigma_kin = 1.5 m/s (accommodates allowable tire slip and chassis compliance).

5.3 Acceleration Stability & Dynamic Jitter Score (S_stab)
Detects high-frequency GNSS noise and numerical jump artifacts by evaluating the discrepancy
between differentiated GNSS velocity and onboard longitudinal IMU acceleration:

   D_acc(t) = | (v_gnss(t) - v_gnss(t - delta_t)) / delta_t - a_long(t) |
   S_stab(t) = exp( - D_acc(t)^2 / (2 * sigma_acc^2) )

   Parameter: sigma_acc = 2.0 m/s^2.

5.4 Continuous Composite Reliability Metric (Q_t)
The multi-sensor consistency scores are fused via a convex combination:

   Q_t = w_1 * S_sat(t) + w_2 * S_kin(t) + w_3 * S_stab(t)

   Weights: w_1 = 0.45, w_2 = 0.35, w_3 = 0.20 (sum = 1.0).
   Output Range: Q_t in [0.0, 1.0]. Q_t = 1.0 indicates pristine open sky; Q_t < 0.70 signals
   degradation; Q_t < 0.20 signals critical sensor failure.

5.5 Supervised Degradation Labeling Rule
Ground-truth degradation at epoch t is assigned deterministically by:
   Degraded(t) = 1  if (Q_t < 0.70  OR  N_sat < 4  OR  D_kin > 2.0 m/s), else 0

The forward prediction target over forecast horizon H (in seconds) is:
   Y(t, H) = max_{tau in (t, t + H]} Degraded(tau)

6. MACHINE LEARNING MODELS & TRAINING SPECIFICATIONS
================================================================================
6.1 Causal Observation Feature Vector (s_t in R^21)
At each epoch t, a 21-dimensional causal feature vector is constructed from historical
measurements over a 5.0 s window (L = 50 epochs at 10 Hz):
   s_t = [ Q_t,  N_sat,  D_kin,  v_gnss,  v_wheel,
           dQ/dt,  Var(Q, 2s),  Min(Q, 5s),  Mean(Q, 2s),
           dN_sat/dt,  Min(N_sat, 5s),
           dD_kin/dt,  Max(D_kin, 2s),
           a_long,  a_lat,  omega_z,  delta_steer,
           dv_wheel/dt,  v_wheel^2,  Q_t * v_wheel,  N_sat / (GDOP_proxy + 1e-4) ]^T

6.2 Models Evaluated
Four classification architectures were trained on V-S1, tuned on V-S2, and tested on V-S3a:
1. Heuristic Persistence Baseline: Assumes future state equals instantaneous degraded state.
2. L2-Regularized Logistic Regression: Baseline linear decision boundary with balanced class weights.
3. Random Forest Classifier: Ensemble of bagging decision trees.
4. Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost): Sequential tree boosting optimizing log-loss.

6.3 Detailed Hyperparameters and Training Regimes
- Logistic Regression:
  * Regularization: L2 penalty, inverse strength C = 1.0, solver = 'lbfgs', max_iter = 1000.
  * Class Weighting: Balanced inverse frequency weighting.
- Random Forest:
  * Number of Trees: 100 estimators.
  * Max Tree Depth: 12.
  * Min Samples per Leaf: 5.
  * Split Criterion: Gini impurity; max features = 'sqrt'.
- Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost - Primary Model):
  * Number of Estimators (Trees): 150.
  * Maximum Tree Depth: 6.
  * Learning Rate (Eta): 0.05.
  * Subsample Ratio: 0.80 (row sampling per tree).
  * Colsample by Tree: 0.80 (feature sampling per tree).
  * Objective Function: 'binary:logistic'.
  * Regularization: L1 (alpha) = 0.1, L2 (lambda) = 1.0.

6.4 Probability Calibration via Platt Scaling & ECE Verification
To ensure raw classifier margins f(s_t) represent true posterior probabilities:
   P(Y = 1 | s_t) = 1 / (1 + exp( - (A * f(s_t) + B) ))

Scalar parameters A and B were fitted via maximum likelihood on validation split V-S2.
Calibration quality was verified using Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across M = 10 bins:
   ECE = sum_{m=1}^M (|B_m| / N) * | acc(B_m) - conf(B_m) |

Empirical Results on Held-Out Test Split V-S3a:
- Uncalibrated XGBoost ECE: 0.084.
- Platt-Calibrated XGBoost ECE: 0.021 (a 75.0% reduction in calibration error).
- Discrimination Performance: AUC-ROC = 0.942, Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC) = 0.812.

6.5 Degradation Prediction Lead Time Distribution
The classifier reliably anticipates oncoming degradation prior to actual signal loss:
- Mean Warning Lead Time: 3.65 seconds.
- Median Warning Lead Time: 3.80 seconds.
- 90th Percentile Lead Time: 4.80 seconds.
This multi-second advance warning provides the executive policy ample time to plan
smooth mode handovers before corrupted pseudoranges penetrate the state estimator.

7. STRAPDOWN DEAD RECKONING & IMU MECHANIZATION
================================================================================
7.1 Strapdown Kinematic Heading & Position Integration
When operating in Dead Reckoning mode (Action A_3), position is propagated purely through
chassis kinematics and inertial measurements, completely decoupled from GNSS:
   psi_dr(k) = psi_dr(k-1) + (omega_z(k) - b_g) * delta_t
   v_dr(k)   = v_wheel(k)
   p_e,dr(k) = p_e,dr(k-1) + v_dr(k) * cos(psi_dr(k)) * delta_t
   p_n,dr(k) = p_n,dr(k-1) + v_dr(k) * sin(psi_dr(k)) * delta_t

7.2 Accelerometer & Gyroscope Bias Formulations
Raw IMU measurements are compensated online:
   a_corr = a_raw - b_a
   omega_corr = omega_raw - b_g
where b_g is the online estimated gyroscope bias tracked by the EKF during HYBRID operation.
During DR outages, b_g is frozen at its last estimated value.

7.3 Wheel-Odometry Speed Blending & Slip Compensation
Forward longitudinal velocity v_wheel is obtained from the average of rear-wheel CAN pulse
sensors, avoiding front-wheel steering scrub effects. Slip ratio is bounded through
acceleration consistency checks with IMU longitudinal specific force.


================================================================================
8. 6-STATE LOOSELY-COUPLED EXTENDED KALMAN FILTER (EKF)
================================================================================
8.1 State Vector & Non-Linear Propagation Model
The central fusion core is a 6-state loosely-coupled error-state EKF:
   x_k = [ p_e(k),  p_n(k),  v_e(k),  v_n(k),  psi(k),  b_g(k) ]^T in R^6

Discrete Kinematic Process Model:
Given input vector u_k = [a_long(k), a_lat(k), omega_z(k)]^T:
   p_e(k) = p_e(k-1) + v_e(k-1) * delta_t
   p_n(k) = p_n(k-1) + v_n(k-1) * delta_t
   v_e(k) = v_e(k-1) + (a_long * cos(psi) - a_lat * sin(psi)) * delta_t
   v_n(k) = v_n(k-1) + (a_long * sin(psi) + a_lat * cos(psi)) * delta_t
   psi(k) = psi(k-1) + (omega_z - b_g) * delta_t
   b_g(k) = b_g(k-1)

8.2 State Transition Jacobian Matrix Formulation
The Jacobian matrix F_k = df / dx evaluated at state x_{k-1} is:

   F_k = [ 1   0   delta_t     0                     0                     0        ]
         [ 0   1      0     delta_t                  0                     0        ]
         [ 0   0      1        0      (-a_long*sin(psi) - a_lat*cos(psi))*dt   0        ]
         [ 0   0      0        1       (a_long*cos(psi) - a_lat*sin(psi))*dt   0        ]
         [ 0   0      0        0                     1                  -delta_t    ]
         [ 0   0      0        0                     0                     1        ]

8.3 Process Noise Covariance Matrix Structure
The process noise covariance matrix Q in R^{6x6} is formulated as:
   Q = diag([ sigma_p^2, sigma_p^2, sigma_v^2, sigma_v^2, sigma_psi^2, sigma_bg^2 ])

Empirically tuned values:
- Position noise: sigma_p = 0.01 m
- Velocity noise: sigma_v = 0.10 m/s
- Heading noise: sigma_psi = 0.005 rad
- Gyro bias random walk: sigma_bg = 0.0001 rad/s

A priori covariance propagation:
   P_k^- = F_k * P_{k-1} * F_k^T + Q

8.4 Quality-Adaptive Measurement Covariance Inflation (R_k)
GNSS observations provide horizontal position and velocity:
   z_k = [ p_e^gnss, p_n^gnss, v_e^gnss, v_n^gnss ]^T = H * x_k + v_k
where observation matrix H in R^{4x6} maps states 1-4 directly.

Crucially, observation noise covariance R_k dynamically scales with composite quality Q_k:
   R_k = R_nominal * ( 1.0 + gamma_scale * ((1.0 - Q_k) / (Q_k + epsilon))^2 )

Parameters:
- Nominal Covariance: R_nominal = diag([2.25 m^2, 2.25 m^2, 0.0625 (m/s)^2, 0.0625 (m/s)^2])
- Scaling Aggressiveness: gamma_scale = 5.0
- Singularity Epsilon: epsilon = 1e-4

Under pristine conditions (Q_k = 1.0), R_k = R_nominal. When degradation begins (Q_k = 0.3),
R_k inflates by a factor of ~28x, smoothly shifting filter reliance onto dead reckoning
without discrete discontinuities.

8.5 Kalman Gain & A Posteriori Updates
   K_k = P_k^- * H^T * ( H * P_k^- * H^T + R_k )^(-1)
   x_hat_k = x_hat_k^- + K_k * ( z_k - H * x_hat_k^- )
   P_k = ( I - K_k * H ) * P_k^-

9. ANALYTICAL DEAD RECKONING UNCERTAINTY & SURVIVABILITY
================================================================================
9.1 Continuous-Time Covariance Propagation (Lyapunov Equation)
During complete GNSS denial, position variance evolves according to the continuous-time
differential Lyapunov equation:
   d P_pos / dt = 2 * P_vel + v(t)^2 * sigma_psi^2(t) * I_{2x2}

9.2 4th-Order Polynomial Horizontal Variance Expansion
Integrating across outage duration T under the assumption of white gyroscope rate noise
(sigma_psi^2(t) = sigma_{psi, 0}^2 + sigma_omega^2 * t) yields the exact 4th-order polynomial
horizontal variance expansion:

   sigma_pos^2(T) = sigma_{pos, 0}^2 + sigma_v^2 * T^2
                    + (1/3) * v^2 * sigma_{psi, 0}^2 * T^2
                    + (1/12) * v^2 * sigma_omega^2 * T^4

Physical Parameters:
- sigma_{pos, 0}^2: Initial horizontal position variance at outage onset (trace(P_k[0:2, 0:2])).
- sigma_v: Longitudinal velocity odometry noise (~0.15 m/s).
- sigma_{psi, 0}^2: Initial heading uncertainty at outage onset (~0.05 rad^2).
- sigma_omega: Gyroscope rate noise density & drift rate (~0.015 rad/s).
- v: Vehicle longitudinal speed (m/s).
- T: Elapsed outage duration (seconds).

Notice the T^4 dependence: for small T, drift is dominated by quadratic velocity noise;
for prolonged T, gyroscope drift multiplied by forward velocity causes quartic runaway.

9.3 Rayleigh Error Distribution & Safety Bound Formulation
Horizontal localization error ||p_dr - p_ref||_2 is modeled as a Rayleigh distribution:
   S(T, E_thresh) = 1 - exp( - E_thresh^2 / (2 * sigma_pos^2(T)) )

where E_thresh = 5.0 m represents the operational lane-level safety bound.

9.4 Closed-Form Bi-Quadratic Survivable Duration Derivation (T_surv)
The median survivable duration T_surv is the time required for failure probability to reach 50%
(S(T_surv, E_thresh) = 0.5):
   1 - exp( - E_thresh^2 / (2 * sigma_target^2) ) = 0.5
   ==> sigma_target^2 = E_thresh^2 / (2 * ln(2)) = 25.0 / (2 * 0.693147) = 18.0337 m^2

Equating sigma_pos^2(T) = sigma_target^2 yields a bi-quadratic polynomial in u = T^2:
   a * u^2 + b * u + c = 0

where polynomial coefficients are:
   a = (1/12) * v^2 * sigma_omega^2
   b = sigma_v^2 + (1/3) * v^2 * sigma_{psi, 0}^2
   c = sigma_{pos, 0}^2 - sigma_target^2

The exact closed-form survivable duration T_surv is computed analytically:
   If sigma_{pos, 0}^2 >= sigma_target^2:
      T_surv = 0.0 seconds (system already exceeds variance budget)
   Else:
      T_surv = sqrt( ( -b + sqrt(b^2 - 4 * a * c) ) / (2 * a) )

This closed-form formulation executes in < 0.05 microseconds, providing an instantaneous
deterministic horizon limit for the decision policy.

10. ACTION-CONDITIONED ERROR FORECASTING ENGINE
================================================================================
10.1 Counterfactual Formulation: Beyond Degraded State Detection
Conventional navigation systems switch based solely on current signal state. VYRA implements
an action-conditioned regressor that predicts the future consequence of each candidate action:
   e_hat(t, A, H) = f_theta( x_cond(t, A, H) ) ~= max_{tau in (t, t + H]} || p_A(tau) - p_ref(tau) ||_2

evaluated across actions A in {GNSS, HYBRID, DR} and horizons H in {1.0s, 3.0s, 5.0s, 10.0s}.

10.2 Interaction Feature Vector Design (x_cond in R^34)
To enable tree-based algorithms to model physical non-linearities and coupling terms,
x_cond in R^34 appends explicit bilinear cross-terms:
   - Base Causal Feature Vector: s_t (21 dimensions)
   - One-Hot Action Encoding: a_A in {0, 1}^3 (3 dimensions)
   - Forecast Horizon: H in seconds (1 dimension)
   - Action-Horizon Interaction: a_A * H (3 dimensions)
   - Action-Quality Bilinear Interactions: a_A (x) [ Q_t, N_sat, D_kin ] (6 dimensions)
   Total Dimension = 21 + 3 + 1 + 3 + 6 = 34 dimensions.

10.3 Offline Target Construction Across Candidate Actions
During offline training on split V-S1, counterfactual trajectories were generated for all
three candidate actions:
- GNSS Consequence: Raw GPS fixes (with zero-order hold during simulated outages).
- HYBRID Consequence: Filtered state of the 6-state EKF.
- DR Consequence: Open-loop strapdown dead reckoning integrated from epoch t.
The target e_max^true(t, A, H) was extracted causally via reversed rolling max windows.
These future targets are strictly inaccessible during online inference.

10.4 Supervised Loss Function: Robust Huber Loss Formulation
The action-conditioned regression ensemble was trained by minimizing Huber loss (delta = 1.0 m):
   L(theta) = (1 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N ell_delta( e_hat_i - e_max,i^true )
where ell_delta(r) = 0.5 * r^2 if |r| <= delta, else delta * (|r| - 0.5 * delta).
Huber loss prevents severe outlier pseudorange multipath spikes from destabilizing gradient updates.

10.5 Offline Oracle Decision Regret & Ranking Accuracy
Evaluated against an offline exhaustive oracle on held-out test split V-S3a:
- Top-1 Ranking Accuracy: 95.43% (the action predicted to be best matched the true best action).
- Mean Decision Regret: 0.056 m (the excess error incurred by choosing the predicted best
  action versus the true optimal action was only 5.6 centimeters).
- Spearman Rank Correlation: rho = 0.8225 (H = 5.0 s), confirming strong monotonic ranking order.


================================================================================
11. MULTI-OBJECTIVE ADAPTIVE DECISION POLICY
================================================================================
11.1 Multi-Objective Cost Function Formulation (J(A))
At each 10 Hz epoch, decision cost J(A) is evaluated for every candidate action A in {GNSS, HYBRID, DR}:

   J(A) = e_hat(t, A, H) + beta * E_thresh * P_hat_viol(t, A, H)
          + lambda_switch * I(A != M_{t-1}) + Pi_DR(A)

Parameters & Components:
- e_hat(t, A, H): Predicted maximum horizontal error in meters.
- beta = 2.0: Risk-aversion penalty weight on safety threshold breach.
- E_thresh = 5.0 m: Operational safety bound.
- P_hat_viol(t, A, H) = I( e_hat(t, A, H) > E_thresh ): Predicted violation indicator.
- lambda_switch = 1.0 m: Switching penalty discouraging frivolous handovers.
- I(A != M_{t-1}): Indicator function taking 1 if candidate action changes the current active mode.

11.2 Dead-Reckoning Survivability Penalty Function
Prevents the vehicle from committing to Dead Reckoning if the duration will exceed survivability:
   If A = DR and T_surv < H:
      Pi_DR(A) = kappa_surv * [ 1.0 + (H - T_surv) ]
   Else:
      Pi_DR(A) = 0.0

   Parameter: kappa_surv = 10.0 m.

11.3 Regularization Mechanisms: Hysteresis Margin & Minimum Dwell Time
To prevent mode chattering and stabilize filter transitions:
1. Candidate Selection: A^* = argmin_{A in A} J(A)
2. Hysteresis Margin Rule: A transition from current mode M_{t-1} to candidate A^* occurs
   if and only if:
      J(M_{t-1}) - J(A^*) > epsilon_hyst,   where epsilon_hyst = 0.5 m
3. Minimum Dwell Time Constraint: Once a mode is selected, transitions are inhibited until
   elapsed continuous residence time satisfies:
      delta_t_dwell >= tau_dwell,   where tau_dwell = 2.0 s (20 epochs at 10 Hz)

11.4 Emergency Safety Override Interlocks
If catastrophic divergence is predicted, the dwell-time lock is immediately bypassed:
   Override(t) = 1  if ( e_hat(t, M_{t-1}, H) > e_emerg  OR  Q_t < Q_crit )
   Parameters: e_emerg = 15.0 m; Q_crit = 0.20.

14. FAILURE MODE ANALYSIS, DIAGNOSTICS & SYSTEM LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
14.1 Reacquisition Discontinuity Following Prolonged Denial
When satellite visibility is abruptly restored after an extended outage (> 20 s), the
accumulated open-loop DR drift creates an instantaneous spatial discrepancy between the EKF
prior and the incoming GNSS fix. To prevent innovation spikes from destabilizing the filter,
VYRA employs a smooth re-convergence gain that dampens the initial update step.

14.2 High-Yaw Dynamic Extrapolation Drift
During sharp, rapid cornering maneuvers coinciding with satellite denial, non-linear tire
scrub and transient chassis lateral acceleration introduce second-order kinematics that
exceed planar 2D assumptions. In these regimes, the analytical uncertainty expansion
sigma_pos^2(T) conservatively shortens T_surv, forcing earlier alerts.

14.3 Sensor Biases in Outages Exceeding 30 Seconds
For outages beyond 30 seconds, open-loop gyroscope drift integration exhibits quartic
variance growth (T^4). Without external aiding (e.g. Visual Odometry or magnetic compass),
no dead-reckoning system can maintain < 5 m bounds indefinitely. VYRA's survivability
engine flags this condition early, allowing the vehicle motion planner to initiate a
safe controlled stop.

14.4 Theoretical & Practical Limitations
1. Software-Simulated Outages: Outages were injected via software masking onto recorded
   benchmark runs. While reproducing real signal loss, secondary RF analog effects
   (receiver oscillator phase jumps) were not present.
2. Single Vehicle Class: The IO-VNBD dataset utilizes passenger sedan dynamics. Heavy trucks
   or articulated vehicles with different tire-road compliance will require re-tuned noise parameters.
3. Planar Navigation: Horizontal (East-North) localization was evaluated; vertical (altitude)
   estimation was decoupled.
